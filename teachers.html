FOR TEACHERS, TUTORS, AND READING SPECIALISTS

An Agency in the classroom.

Ten minutes a day, one case per detective, no grading. Built for the reading block, the center rotation, and the child who finished early and is reading under the desk.

Where it fits

Independent reading response

Replace the reading log. A case file takes ten minutes, asks for one piece of evidence and three to five lines, and ends with a stamp instead of a signature. Detectives choose their own book and their own card.

A literacy center

One sand timer, one card box, a stack of case files, a hole punch. The center runs itself once the rules page has been read aloud once. The color on the card tells the detective what to do, so the center needs no adult.

Early finishers and advanced readers

For the child who reads two grades ahead, the three-dot cards stretch without changing the book. Curriculum compacting research shows advanced readers lose nothing when routine work is replaced with work at their level. The Agency is the replacement.

Reluctant and struggling writers

The Fairness Clause is written into the rules: on a hard day a detective may draw and label, dictate while a grown-up scribes, or pick a one-dot card. Rank counts files, so a dictated file counts fully. No child is ever behind in the Agency.

Card colors and the standards

The deck has thirty six prompts in four colors. Each color is a strand. Dots mark the stretch: one dot sits in the kindergarten to first grade band, two dots in second, three in third. The mapping below uses the Common Core anchor strands, which the North Carolina Standard Course of Study and most state standards parallel.

The Agency is a game and makes no claim to cover a standard on its own. A card is a prompt, a file is evidence of practice, and the teacher decides what either one shows.

Printing and setting up

The kit is sixteen letter pages as a PDF. Black ink prints fine. For a classroom:

  • Print the card sheets on 65 lb cardstock and cut on the dashed lines. Laminate if the box will see thirty hands a day.
  • Print one Agency Rules page per class, read it aloud, and have every detective sign it. The Fairness Clause is written by the class.
  • Print case files in a stack of fifty. The detectives punch their own.
  • One binder per detective, or one class binder with tab dividers by name.
  • Print the badge page once per detective. Badges go in the binder's front pocket as ranks arrive.
